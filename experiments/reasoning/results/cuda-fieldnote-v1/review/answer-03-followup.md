# Harbor Water Pilot Selection Brief

## Executive Summary

**Selection:** Cedar system  
**Daily operating cost:** 18 credits  
**Orange route bypass:** UV chamber

---

## Detailed Findings

### 1. Capacity Compliance: Cedar Qualifies, Birch Does Not

The council requires **at least 1000 liters per day** [audit.md]. The 30-day trial measured:

- **Cedar:** 1200 liters per day [trial.md]
- **Birch:** 900 liters per day [trial.md]

**Critical correction:** An early brochure claimed Birch delivered 1400 liters per day, but the independent audit corrected this to 900 liters per day, noting the brochure's "short clean-water test was not representative" [audit.md].

**Conclusion:** Cedar alone meets the council's minimum requirement (1200 > 1000); Birch alone fails (900 < 1000).

### 2. Daily Operating Cost: Cedar = 18 credits

The operating ledger reports:

- **Cedar:** 18 credits per day [cost.md]
- **Birch:** 12 credits per day [cost.md]

Cedar requires a 400-credit inlet upgrade; Birch requires no inlet upgrade. However, the grant covers installation but **does not cover recurring operating costs** [cost.md].

### 3. Orange Route Bypass: UV Chamber

The routing diagram on page 2 shows:

- **Blue route (normal treatment):** Intake → UV chamber → Storage
- **Orange route (maintenance bypass):** Intake → [skips UV chamber] → Storage

The orange dashed line bypasses the **UV chamber** component [Harbor Water Pilot page 2].

---

## Maintenance and Reliability Caveats

- Cedar requires filter inspection every 7 days; Birch every 14 days [maintenance.md]
- The pilot measured **no winter performance** [maintenance.md]
- Neither system has established annual reliability from this 30-day pilot [maintenance.md]

**Important:** Do not treat a 30-day result as an annual guarantee [maintenance.md].
