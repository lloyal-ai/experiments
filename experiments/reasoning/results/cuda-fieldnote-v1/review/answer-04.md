# Harbor Water Pilot Selection Report

## Thesis

The council should select the **Cedar pilot system** as the only option meeting the 1000 liters/day capacity requirement, despite its higher energy consumption and operating costs. While Birch offers lower operating expenses (12 credits/day vs. 18 credits/day for Cedar), it fails the minimum capacity threshold (900 liters/day vs. 1000 liters/day requirement) and requires council intervention to meet demand. Both systems remain untested in winter conditions, representing a significant reliability gap regardless of selection.

## Supporting Evidence: Capacity Requirements Dictate Selection

The decisive factor is audited daily capacity, which directly determines feasibility. The 30-day independent audit measured Cedar at **1200 liters per day** and Birch at **900 liters per day** [trial.md](attachment://trial.md). This represents a 33% capacity difference (1200 vs. 900 liters/day) that is material to the council's requirement of **at least 1000 liters per day** [audit.md](attachment://audit.md).

Birch alone fails this requirement (900 < 1000), necessitating either a second system installation or infrastructure expansion—costs not covered by the grant. Cedar alone meets the requirement (1200 > 1000), making it the only feasible single-system option.

The Birch brochure claim of 1400 liters/day was inflated and corrected to 900 liters/day following the audit [audit.md](attachment://audit.md). The brochure used a short clean-water test that is not representative of actual conditions, suggesting the council should reject marketing claims in favor of audited performance data.

## Complicating Evidence: Energy, Cost, and Maintenance Tradeoffs

**Energy Consumption:** Cedar uses **6 kWh per day** versus Birch's **3 kWh per day**, meaning Cedar consumes 100% more energy [trial.md](attachment://trial.md). This represents a significant operational tradeoff that the council must weigh against capacity needs.

**Operating Costs:** Cedar costs **18 credits per day** to operate versus Birch's **12 credits per day**, excluding installation [cost.md](attachment://cost.md/page/1). Cedar's 6 credits/day higher operating cost reflects its greater energy consumption and likely more complex operation.

**Installation Requirements:** Cedar requires a **400-credit inlet upgrade** while Birch needs no inlet upgrade [cost.md](attachment://cost.md/page/1). The grant covers installation costs but does not cover recurring operating costs, meaning the council will bear the long-term operating expense difference.

**Maintenance Intervals:** Cedar requires filter inspection every **7 days** versus Birch's **14 days** [maintenance.md](file:///content/maintenance.md). This means Cedar requires twice the maintenance labor and potentially more frequent parts replacement.

## Resolution: Capacity Requirement Trumps All Other Factors

The resolution is that capacity requirements are a hard constraint that cannot be compromised. While Cedar's higher energy use, operating costs, and maintenance frequency create financial and operational burdens, these are secondary to the fundamental requirement that the system must deliver 1000+ liters/day.

The council faces three options:
1. **Select Cedar alone** (1200 liters/day) — meets requirement, higher costs
2. **Select Birch + additional infrastructure** (900 liters/day + expansion) — fails requirement unless expanded
3. **Select both systems** (2100 liters/day) — exceeds requirement, potentially wasteful

Option 1 is the only straightforward path to meeting the council's requirement without additional capital expenditure. The 400-credit inlet upgrade for Cedar is covered by the grant, reducing the initial barrier to deployment.

## Prescription: Select Cedar with Winter Reliability Caveat

The council should proceed with the **Cedar pilot system** as the sole water treatment option. This selection is predicated on the assumption that winter reliability is acceptable given the current evidence limitations.

**Implementation:**
- Cedar requires only a 400-credit inlet upgrade (grant-covered)
- Operating costs of 18 credits/day are sustainable if the council can absorb the energy premium
- 7-day filter inspection intervals require established maintenance procedures

**Critical Caveat:** Both systems lack winter performance data. The pilot measured **no winter performance** and neither system has established annual reliability [maintenance.md](file:///content/maintenance.md). The 30-day trial period is insufficient to guarantee performance under varied seasonal conditions, particularly cold weather operations.

The council should:
1. Monitor Cedar's performance over multiple seasons before full deployment
2. Require winter reliability testing before scaling the pilot to full capacity
3. Budget for potential maintenance cost increases during winter months

## Conclusion

The council must select the **Cedar water pilot system** because it is the only option that meets the 1000 liters/day capacity requirement on its own. While Birch offers lower operating costs (12 credits/day vs. 18 credits/day) and lower energy use (3 kWh/day vs. 6 kWh/day), it fails the minimum capacity threshold (900 liters/day vs. 1000 liters/day requirement).

The selection of Cedar is justified despite higher energy consumption, operating costs, and more frequent maintenance intervals. The 400-credit inlet upgrade is grant-covered, and the system's 1200 liters/day capacity provides a 200-liter/day buffer above the requirement.

### Limitations

**Winter Reliability:** Neither system has winter performance data. The pilot measured no winter performance and neither system has established annual reliability [maintenance.md](file:///content/maintenance.md). This represents the most significant unknown in the selection decision.

**Annual Performance Guarantees:** The 30-day trial period is insufficient to guarantee performance under varied seasonal conditions [maintenance.md](file:///content/maintenance.md). Long-term maintenance cost projections remain unverified.

**Annual Reliability Data:** No annual performance guarantees are available from this short-term pilot [maintenance.md](file:///content/maintenance.md).

**Winter Reliability Data:** No winter reliability data exists in the pilot evidence [maintenance.md](file:///content/maintenance.md).
