# Harbor Water Pilot Selection Analysis

## Thesis

**Cedar is the only feasible option for the council because it is the only system that meets the capacity requirement of 1000 liters per day; Birch alone fails at 900 liters per day despite its lower operating cost and energy use.** This conclusion rests on the hard constraint that the council's capacity requirement must be satisfied, which the 30-day independent audit definitively established [trial](https://example.com/trial.md).

## Supporting Evidence: Capacity Constraint is Non-Negotiable

The independent 30-day trial and audit measured the following audited capacities [trial](https://example.com/trial.md):

- **Cedar**: 1200 liters per day
- **Birch**: 900 liters per day

The council requires at least 1000 liters per day, so Cedar meets the requirement while Birch alone fails [audit](https://example.com/audit.md). An early brochure claimed Birch delivered 1400 liters per day, but the independent audit corrected that number to 900 liters per day [audit](https://example.com/audit.md). The brochure used a short clean-water test that is not representative of real conditions.

This capacity gap is the decisive factor: Cedar alone meets the requirement at 1200 liters per day, whereas Birch alone fails at 900 liters per day. The trial results show Cedar delivered 1200 liters per day and Birch delivered 900 liters per day over 30 days, both meeting the drinking-water quality target [trial](https://example.com/trial.md).

## Complicating Evidence: Cost and Operational Tradeoffs

The operating ledger reports Cedar at 18 credits per day and Birch at 12 credits per day, excluding installation [cost](https://example.com/cost.md). Cedar needs a 400-credit inlet upgrade; Birch requires no inlet upgrade [cost](https://example.com/cost.md). A grant covers installation but does not cover recurring operating costs [cost](https://example.com/cost.md).

Energy consumption under the same operating conditions shows Cedar uses 6 kWh per day and Birch uses 3 kWh per day [trial](https://example.com/trial.md). The brochure's claim of 1400 liters per day for Birch was corrected to 900 liters per day, confirming the test was not representative [audit](https://example.com/audit.md).

## Resolution: Capacity Constraint Trumps Cost Considerations

The operating costs (18 credits/day for Cedar vs 12 credits/day for Birch) are from the ledger excluding installation. The installation costs are separate (400 credits for Cedar inlet upgrade vs none for Birch). The council requirement of 1000 liters/day is not met by Birch alone (900 liters/day per audit) [audit](https://example.com/audit.md). Cedar alone meets the requirement at 1200 liters/day [trial](https://example.com/trial.md).

Cedar alone is the feasible option because it meets the council's capacity requirement of 1000 liters per day (1200 liters/day audited), whereas Birch alone fails at 900 liters/day [cost](https://example.com/cost.md). The council must decide whether the 400-credit inlet upgrade for Cedar is within budget, given that a grant covers installation but not recurring operating costs [cost](https://example.com/cost.md).

## Maintenance and Reliability Limitations

Cedar requires filter inspection every 7 days; Birch requires filter inspection every 14 days [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md). The pilot measured only 30 days of operation [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md). The pilot measured no winter performance [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md). Neither system has established annual reliability from this short pilot [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md).

The pilot measured only 30 days of operation and the pilot measured no winter performance [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md). Winter performance was explicitly NOT measured by the pilot [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md). No data exists on winter reliability for either Cedar or Birch systems [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md). The 30-day trial conducted under unspecified "same operating conditions" does not represent winter conditions [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md). Annual reliability cannot be established from this short pilot duration [maintenance](https://example.com/maintenance.md).

## Prescription: Select Cedar with Budget Caveat

Select Cedar because it is the only system that meets the council's capacity requirement of 1000 liters per day. The council must budget for the 400-credit inlet upgrade for Cedar, which is not covered by the grant [cost](https://example.com/cost.md). If budget constraints are severe, Birch may be infeasible unless combined with another source, but the evidence does not support combining systems [cost](https://example.com/cost.md).

## Conclusion

Cedar is the only feasible option for the council because it is the only system that meets the capacity requirement of 1000 liters per day; Birch alone fails at 900 liters per day despite its lower operating cost and energy use [trial](https://example.com/trial.md). The capacity constraint is the hard constraint that determines feasibility, with cost and maintenance being secondary considerations.
