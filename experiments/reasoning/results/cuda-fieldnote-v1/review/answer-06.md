# Harbor Water Pilot System Selection Report

## Thesis

**Cedar should be selected for the Harbor water pilot** because it is the only system meeting the council's 1000-liter daily capacity requirement, despite requiring a 400-credit inlet upgrade. The 30-day trial demonstrates Cedar delivers 1200 liters per day versus Birch's 900 liters per day, which fails the minimum threshold. While Birch requires no installation upgrade, its capacity shortfall is a hard constraint that cannot be resolved through operational adjustments.

## Capacity and Energy Efficiency: The Hard Constraint

The independent 30-day audit corrected the early brochure claim that Birch delivered 1400 liters per day to 900 liters per day. The brochure used a short clean-water test that is not representative of actual conditions. The council requires at least 1000 liters per day, so Birch alone fails the capacity requirement.

The trial results show Cedar delivered 1200 liters per day and Birch delivered 900 liters per day over 30 days, under the same operating conditions. The critical finding is that Cedar outperforms Birch in both capacity and energy efficiency. Cedar uses 6 kWh per day versus Birch's 3 kWh per day, but Cedar's higher capacity justifies the additional energy consumption.

**Important Distinction:** The distractor.md file warns that lighting quantities must not be used as water production or energy figures. The early brochure's 1400 figure for Birch was likely confused with unrelated lighting pilot data (1400 lumens for Cedar lamps and 1200 lumens for Birch lamps).

## Operating Costs and Installation Constraints

The operating ledger reports Cedar at 18 credits per day and Birch at 12 credits per day, excluding installation costs. The grant covers installation but does not cover recurring operating costs.

**Feasible Option:** Cedar alone meets the council's 1000-liter/day requirement (1200 liters per day in trial), whereas Birch alone fails (900 liters per day). However, Cedar requires a 400-credit inlet upgrade. The feasible option is Cedar, provided the council can fund the installation upgrade.

The trial results confirm that Cedar outperforms Birch in both capacity and energy efficiency. Cedar meets the 1000-liter daily requirement, while Birch does not. The operating costs in the ledger (18 credits/day for Cedar, 12 credits/day for Birch) exclude installation costs. The ledger reflects ongoing operational expenses, while the installation requirements (400-credit for Cedar, none for Birch) are capital expenditures covered by the grant.

## Maintenance and Reliability: The Unknowns

Cedar requires filter inspection every 7 days versus Birch's every 14 days. However, the 30-day trial measured no winter performance. Neither system has established annual reliability from this short pilot. Do not treat a 30-day result as an annual guarantee.

**What is Unknown:** Winter reliability is completely unknown — the pilot measured "no winter performance." Annual reliability has not been established for either system. Long-term maintenance intervals beyond the 7-day/14-day filter inspections are not specified. Performance under extended cold-weather conditions cannot be determined from this trial.

## Visual Routing Explanation

The Harbor Water Pilot Figure 1 on page 2 shows two distinct water treatment routing paths. The blue route (normal treatment route) flows from the Intake component through the UV chamber to the Storage component, following the standard treatment sequence. The orange route (maintenance bypass) provides an alternative path from the Intake component directly to the Storage component, bypassing the UV chamber entirely. This bypass allows water to flow from Intake to Storage without undergoing UV treatment, which would be used during maintenance periods when the UV chamber needs servicing or repair.

## Resolution and Recommendation

The capacity requirement creates a binary decision: Cedar meets the 1000-liter/day threshold; Birch does not. The installation cost for Cedar is a capital expense covered by the grant, not a recurring operational burden. The winter reliability unknown represents a risk that should be mitigated through additional testing, but does not change the capacity-based selection.

**Recommendation:** Select Cedar for the Harbor water pilot, contingent on council approval of the 400-credit inlet upgrade. Conduct additional winter reliability testing before full deployment to address the unknown winter performance.

### Limitations

- Winter reliability completely unknown — pilot measured "no winter performance"
- Neither system has established annual reliability from this short pilot
- Long-term maintenance intervals beyond the 7-day/14-day filter inspections are not specified
- Performance under extended cold-weather conditions cannot be determined from this trial
